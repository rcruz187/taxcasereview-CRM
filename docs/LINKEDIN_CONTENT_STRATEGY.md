# Tax Res CRM — LinkedIn Content Strategy
**Version:** 1.0 | **Effective:** August 2026 | **Owner:** Romy Cruz, EA

---

## Purpose

This document governs all LinkedIn content produced for Tax Res CRM. The objective is not engagement for its own sake. Every post must ultimately support one or more of: authority, practitioner trust, SEO traffic, Resource Center traffic, demo requests, qualified leads, or Tax Res CRM awareness among tax resolution firm owners.

This is a customer-acquisition channel. Measure it that way.

## 2. Voice Guidelines — What Sounds Like Romy

Romy Cruz is a practicing Enrolled Agent who built Tax Res CRM out of genuine frustration with software that wasn't designed for resolution work. He is not a startup founder pitching venture capitalists. He is a tax professional talking to other tax professionals.

**Voice characteristics:**
- Direct. Short sentences. No filler.
- Practitioner-to-practitioner, not vendor-to-customer
- Uses specific IRS terminology correctly (CP2000, CSED, RCP, PPIA, CDP, TFRP, 433-A, 433-B)
- Shares actual workflow observations, not generic advice
- Admits what doesn't work before explaining what does
- Never says "I'm excited to announce" or "game-changer" or "synergy"
- Never uses hashtag spam — 2–3 relevant hashtags maximum, only when they add discoverability
- First-person singular, not corporate "we" (exception: "we built" when describing the product)
- Questions at the end are genuine, not performative engagement bait

**What does NOT sound like Romy:**
- "As a thought leader in the tax resolution space..."
- "In today's fast-paced environment..."
- "I'm passionate about helping tax professionals..."
- Lists of 10 items when 4 will do
- Rhetorical questions that answer themselves immediately
- Emojis used as bullet points
- Posts that could have been written by anyone who Googled the topic

## 3. Rules Preventing Generic AI-Sounding Content

The Monday generator produces template drafts. Every generated post must pass these checks before being approved:

1. **Specificity test** — Does the post reference a specific IRS notice type, form number, process name, or real resolution scenario? Generic posts about "IRS debt" or "tax problems" fail this test.

2. **Practitioner test** — Would a tax resolution EA or attorney recognize this as coming from someone who has actually worked cases? If a non-practitioner could have written it, rewrite it.

3. **Source test** — Is every factual claim in the post something Romy can personally attest to from his practice, or is it publicly documented IRS procedure? If neither, remove the claim.

4. **Differentiation test** — Could this exact post be published by Canopy, TaxDome, or a generic tax software company? If yes, it needs a practitioner angle that only Tax Res CRM can credibly own.

5. **Hook test** — Does the opening line create a reason to keep reading? Avoid: "Did you know that..." / "Here are X tips..." / "Tax resolution is complex..."

---

## 4. Prohibited Claims

Never publish without explicit approval from Romy:

- Specific time-savings claims unless from documented internal data ("saves 20 minutes per case")
- Specific acceptance/approval rates for OICs, IAs, or other resolution paths unless citing the current IRS Data Book or official IRS statistics
- Claims that Tax Res CRM integrates with or is approved by the IRS
- Claims about A2A transcript retrieval being "fully automated" — current status is watched-folder import with A2A as the roadmap endpoint; accuracy requires stating the current capability
- Any claim that a specific resolution outcome (OIC acceptance, levy release, etc.) is guaranteed or typical
- Named competitor weaknesses that aren't publicly documented or factually verifiable
- Legal or tax advice framed as applicable to any taxpayer's specific situation
- Revenue or income claims for Tax Res CRM customers
- Claims about Nashville Tax Solutions or any client firm without explicit written consent

---

## 5. Tax/Legal Claim Safeguards

**IRS statistics** — cite the source. IRS Data Book is published annually. OIC acceptance/rejection rates, collection statistics, and enforcement data are public. Use the most recent available year and note it.

**Resolution outcomes** — frame as process description, not outcome guarantee. "The OIC process requires..." not "OICs can eliminate your debt."

**Practitioner advice** — frame as workflow observation, not legal/tax advice. "Here's how most experienced practitioners approach this..." not "You should always..."

**Product capabilities** — describe what exists in production today. Do not describe roadmap features as current unless they are live.

**A2A transcript retrieval specifically** — current production status: Tax Res CRM supports transcript import via watched folder. Full A2A automated pull is on the roadmap and tied to IRS enrollment timelines. Never describe this as "one-click automated" until confirmed live.

## 9. Demo Conversion Rules

Demo posts (5% of rotation) must follow this structure:

1. **Open with a specific problem** a resolution firm has — not a generic tax problem
2. **Show the before state** — what the workflow looks like without Tax Res CRM
3. **Show the after state** — what changes with Tax Res CRM (specific, not vague)
4. **One clear ask** — book a 30-minute walkthrough, link to `/demo`
5. **Never pressure** — "I'd love to show you" not "Don't miss out"

Demo posts must describe a capability that is live in production today.

## 10. Competitor Positioning Rules

**Canopy:**
- Factually supportable: Canopy is built for general tax practices including compliance and resolution. Its workflow is organized around tax filing deadlines and client management.
- Supportable differentiation: Tax Res CRM's workflow is built specifically around IRS enforcement timelines and resolution stages, not filing seasons.
- Never say: Canopy is bad software, Canopy doesn't work, Canopy is overpriced
- Never compare pricing without current verified data from Canopy's public pricing page

**TaxDome:**
- Factually supportable: TaxDome is a broad practice management platform serving all tax practice types.
- Supportable differentiation: Tax Res CRM has IRS-specific features (transcript retrieval, CAF/POA tracking, enforcement timeline views) that aren't standard in general practice management tools.
- Never say: specific feature claims about TaxDome's limitations without verifiable evidence

**Karbon:**
- Karbon targets accounting firms broadly, not tax resolution specifically
- Same positioning approach as TaxDome

**General rule:** Compare workflows, not products. "Software built for compliance workflows requires adaptation for resolution work" is supportable. "Canopy can't do X" requires verification.

**Never name a competitor in a negative context without a specific, verifiable, factual basis.**

## 11. 30-Day Topic Duplication Prevention

The scheduler tracks post bodies from the last 30 days. Before generating a new post:

1. Extract the first significant keyword from the proposed topic
2. Check recent post bodies for that keyword
3. If a recent post covers the same core topic, select an alternative from the topic pool
4. Adjacent topics (e.g., CP2000 and CP2501) are considered different topics
5. The same IRS form covered from a different angle (e.g., "how to respond to CP2000" vs "why the IRS sends CP2000") counts as a different topic

## 12. Hook Variation Rules

Hooks must rotate across these patterns. Never use the same pattern two posts in a row:

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Bold statement | "Most taxpayers who receive a CP2000 notice make one critical mistake." |
| Counter-intuitive | "The IRS rejection rate for OICs is around 67%. Most of those rejections are avoidable." |
| Problem framing | "Here is a workflow problem every tax resolution firm has." |
| Experience open | "I have used Canopy. I have used TaxDome." |
| Process reveal | "Before submitting an OIC, run the IRS math." |
| Specific number | "The IRS collected $68.4 billion through enforcement in FY2023." |
| Question (sparingly) | "What does your firm do when a client gets a CP2000?" |

Avoid:
- "Did you know..."
- "Here are [N] ways to..."
- "As a tax professional..."
- "In my experience..." (too vague — be specific about what experience)
- Starting with "I" two posts in a row

## 13. Post Format Variation

Rotate through these formats. Never run the same format three times in a row:

| Format | Structure | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| List-in-prose | Short intro → 3–5 arrow-prefixed items → close | Practitioner checklists, process steps |
| Narrative | Problem → context → insight → close | Founder story, educational deep-dives |
| Before/After | State A → transition → State B | Product workflow posts |
| Framework | Name the framework → explain each part → apply it | Educational posts on IRS processes |
| Short-form | 4–8 lines, no lists | Punchy observations, single-idea posts |

**Length guidance:**
- Optimal: 150–250 words
- Maximum: 300 words (LinkedIn truncates at ~220 words before "see more")
- The hook must be in the first 2 lines — that's what shows before truncation
- Short-form posts (under 100 words) are acceptable and often outperform long ones

Running 7-day rolling check:

- If the last 3 posts contained no product mention → next product mention is appropriate

## 17. Monday Generation Rules

The `generate_monday_drafts` action runs every Monday at 7:00 AM ET via pg_cron (ID 11).

**Generation process:**
1. Find next Tuesday and Thursday slots
2. Check recent 30 days of posts for category distribution
3. Pick underrepresented category using weighted random (adjusted for recency)
4. Pick topic from that category's pool, avoiding recent keywords
5. Build post body using category template with specific topic filled in
6. Insert as `status='draft'` with correct `scheduled_at` (13:00 UTC = 9:00 AM ET)
7. Send alert email to `romy@taxcasereview.org` listing the drafts

**After generation:**
- Drafts appear in Admin Portal → Command Center → LinkedIn → Content Queue
- Romy reviews and edits the body as needed
- Romy clicks Approve
- Scheduler publishes automatically at scheduled time — no further action required

**If Monday generation produces a post on a topic already covered within 30 days:**
- The scheduler picks an alternative topic
- If all topics in a category were covered recently, it picks from a different category

## 18. Tuesday/Thursday Publishing Rules

The scheduler (pg_cron ID 11, every 15 min) checks for:

```sql
status = 'approved'
AND scheduled_at <= now()
AND retry_count < 3
AND tenant_id = 'a0000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000001'
```

**On finding eligible posts:**
1. Attempt idempotency lock (`status = 'publishing'`)
2. Append UTM params to any taxrescrm.net URLs in the body
3. POST to LinkedIn ugcPosts API
4. On success: `status = 'published'`, store `linkedin_post_id` and `linkedin_url`
5. On failure: increment `retry_count`, revert to `'approved'` if retries remain
6. On permanent failure (retry_count >= 3): `status = 'failed'`, send alert email

**Safety caps:**
- Maximum 3 posts published per cron run
- Only `status='approved'` posts are eligible — drafts are structurally excluded
- `autopilot` flag has no effect on publishing of approved posts

## 19. Weekly Performance Report Structure

Generated every Monday at 7:00 AM ET alongside the new drafts. Stored in `linkedin_weekly_reports`.

**Report sections:**
1. Week dates (Mon–Sun)
2. Posts published (count + list with LinkedIn URLs)
3. Posts failed (count + error summary)
4. Schedule adherence (published within 15 min of scheduled time = pass)
5. Upcoming next week (approved + scheduled posts)
6. Content category breakdown (what % of posts fell into each pillar)
7. UTM note (track at taxrescrm.net/analytics via GA4 — LinkedIn API analytics not yet available)
8. Open items (failed posts, token expiry warnings)

**What the report cannot include until LinkedIn MDP access is approved:**
- Impressions
- Reactions
- Comments
- Reposts
- Clicks from LinkedIn
- Follower growth

**What the report CAN include now:**
- Publish confirmation (post ID + LinkedIn URL)
- Schedule adherence
- UTM campaign names for cross-referencing in GA4
- Category distribution

## 20. Performance Learning Rules

Once LinkedIn MDP analytics access is approved and impression/click data becomes available:

**Data the system will collect:**
- Impressions per post
- Reactions per post
- Comments per post
- Clicks per post (to taxrescrm.net URLs)
- Engagement rate (reactions + comments + reposts / impressions)

**How it improves generation:**
- After 8 weeks of data: category with highest engagement rate gets +5% weight in rotation
- Hook pattern associated with top 25% engagement posts gets higher selection probability
- Topics with zero engagement after 4 weeks get deprioritized
- Day/time performance tracked (Tuesday vs Thursday, confirmed cadence)

**What the system will never do automatically:**
- Change the publishing schedule without Romy's approval
- Increase autopilot mode without Romy's approval
- Remove a pillar from rotation based on short-term data (minimum 12-week window)
- Publish posts that reference specific performance data without verifying the data source

**Until MDP access exists:** the system tracks UTM-attributed traffic via GA4 as a proxy. Demo bookings from LinkedIn UTM source = the closest available conversion signal.
